## **FUNDING AND PROJECT APPROVAL POLICY**

### **Reciprocal Space Station Consortium**

**Adopted by:** Board of the Reciprocal Space Station Consortium  
**Effective Date:** \[Date\]  
**Administered by:** Open Molecular Software Foundation, as Operating Legal Entity for RSS

This Funding and Project Approval Policy (the “**Policy**”) governs how the Reciprocal Space Station Consortium (“**RSS**” or the “**Consortium**”) considers, approves, funds, oversees, and reviews Consortium-Funded Projects and related uses of Consortium-administered resources.

This Policy is adopted pursuant to the Charter of RSS (the “**Charter**”). In the event of conflict, the Charter controls over this Policy, and applicable law, OMSF governing documents, and binding external obligations control over both.

## **1\. Purpose**

RSS is an open-source software consortium for structural biology. This Policy is intended to:

* provide a clear process for proposing and approving Consortium-Funded Projects;  
* ensure that Consortium-administered resources are used to advance the Mission and Strategic Goals of RSS;  
* distinguish ordinary support and maintenance activity from projects requiring formal approval;  
* create a predictable framework for budget approval, project oversight, and reporting;  
* manage conflicts of interest in funding decisions;  
* support timely decision-making without sacrificing accountability; and  
* ensure that funded work remains consistent with RSS’s open-source mission, release commitments, and governance structure.

## **2\. Scope**

This Policy applies to:

* new Consortium-Funded Projects;  
* material expansions of existing Consortium-Funded Projects;  
* major expenditures requiring Board approval under the Charter;  
* allocation of Consortium-administered funds, personnel, or resources to internal or external performers;  
* project-specific support arrangements that are elevated beyond ordinary membership support;  
* fellowships, subawards, contracts, consulting arrangements, and similar funding mechanisms administered through OMSF on behalf of RSS.

This Policy does not require formal Board approval for routine operational spending already authorized within a Board-approved budget and delegated to the Director, unless otherwise specified by the Board.

## **3\. Governing Principles**

### **3.1 Mission Alignment**

Consortium-administered funds and resources shall be used to advance the Mission of RSS and the Strategic Goals adopted by the Board.

### **3.2 Open-Source Orientation**

Consortium-Funded Projects should ordinarily produce outputs that can be released and maintained consistent with the Charter, the Release and Publication Policy, and the Contribution and Intellectual Property Policy.

### **3.3 Clear Authority**

The Board sets strategic direction, approves major allocations, and oversees major funded efforts. The Director manages day-to-day operations and may implement approved spending within delegated authority. Developers retain technical authority as provided in the Charter.

### **3.4 No Automatic Entitlement**

No Member, Partner, Developer, Board member, institution, or affiliated laboratory is entitled to receive Consortium funding or project support merely by virtue of participation in RSS.

### **3.5 Fairness and Transparency**

Funding decisions should be made on stated grounds, with enough documentation to permit responsible oversight.

### **3.6 Conflict Management**

Where funding decisions may benefit individuals, institutions, laboratories, or projects connected to RSS leadership or governance participants, the conflict-of-interest provisions of the Charter must be applied rigorously.

### **3.7 Practical Flexibility**

RSS may support work through different mechanisms, including direct OMSF-managed staffing, contractor arrangements, fellowships, subawards, cloud and infrastructure spending, travel support, or other lawful and mission-consistent arrangements.

## **4\. Categories of Resource Use**

For purposes of this Policy, Consortium-administered resources may be used for categories including:

* salaries or wages for OMSF-employed personnel working on RSS;  
* contractor or consultant engagements;  
* fellowships, stipends, or similar support for trainees or researchers, where permitted;  
* subawards or institutional agreements, where permitted;  
* cloud, compute, storage, CI, hosting, and other technical infrastructure;  
* software services and collaboration tools;  
* workshops, training, meetings, and community activities;  
* documentation, benchmarking, interoperability, and maintenance efforts;  
* travel or dissemination support where mission-consistent and approved.

The Board may refine or expand these categories over time.

## **5\. What Requires Formal Project Approval**

Formal Board approval is required for:

* any new Consortium-Funded Project involving material allocation of Consortium-administered funds, personnel, or resources;  
* any material increase in scope, budget, or duration of an existing Consortium-Funded Project;  
* any multi-year commitment not already authorized by the Board;  
* any project-specific arrangement requested by a Member or Partner that goes beyond ordinary membership benefits and support;  
* any allocation materially benefiting the institution, laboratory, research group, or closely related project of a sitting Board member or the Director;  
* any fellowship, subaward, or external institutional arrangement requiring formal approval under Board or OMSF policy;  
* any expenditure category specifically reserved to the Board by the Charter or a Board-approved budget rule.

Routine work performed within an already approved Consortium-Funded Project does not require repeated Board approval unless the project materially changes.

## **6\. What Does Not Require Formal Project Approval**

Formal Board approval is not required for ordinary operational acts within a Board-approved budget and delegated authority, including, unless otherwise specified:

* routine support to Members consistent with membership benefits;  
* ordinary maintenance, bug fixing, and release management within existing approved workstreams;  
* routine cloud or software-service expenditures within delegated budget authority;  
* ordinary travel, meeting, or minor procurement decisions within delegated limits;  
* small, time-limited exploratory work authorized by the Director within delegated discretion.

The Board may adopt spending thresholds or categories that further clarify this distinction.

## **7\. Project Proposal Process**

### **7.1 Who May Propose**

A proposal for a Consortium-Funded Project may be initiated by:

* the Director;  
* one or more Developers;  
* a Board member;  
* a Member or Partner, through the Director or the Board;  
* OMSF staff, where administrative or legal considerations require formalization of a project structure.

### **7.2 Form of Proposal**

A project proposal need not be long, but it must be documented in writing and should ordinarily include:

* project title or working description;  
* purpose and mission alignment;  
* lead Developer or proposed technical lead;  
* expected outputs;  
* expected beneficiaries or user community;  
* proposed performers or institutions, if known;  
* estimated budget and duration;  
* anticipated staffing or resource needs;  
* expected release/publication posture;  
* any known conflicts of interest;  
* any known legal, institutional, or funding constraints.

A short project memo, board packet, or written email summary may suffice if it covers these points adequately.

### **7.3 Role of the Director**

The Director may help shape, prioritize, combine, defer, or refine proposals before Board consideration.

Where a Member or Partner request exceeds ordinary support, the Director should determine whether it is better treated as:

* ordinary support within current operations;  
* a candidate Consortium-Funded Project for Board consideration; or  
* a matter for a separate written arrangement administered by OMSF.

## **8\. Required Findings for Approval**

Before approving a Consortium-Funded Project, the Board should be satisfied that the project:

* materially advances the Mission or Strategic Goals of RSS;  
* is feasible within available or reasonably anticipated resources;  
* has an identified technical lead or a credible plan to designate one;  
* has a release posture consistent with the Charter and Release and Publication Policy;  
* has an appropriate contribution/IP posture consistent with applicable policy;  
* does not create unmanaged conflict-of-interest concerns;  
* is administratively and legally supportable by OMSF under applicable rules; and  
* is worth the expected use of Consortium resources relative to other priorities.

The Board need not make formal written findings in every case, but the basis for approval should be evident from the record.

## **9\. Required Elements at Approval**

At the time of approval, or before material work begins, each Consortium-Funded Project should have:

* an approved project title or description;  
* a designated lead Developer or technical lead;  
* an approved budget or budget cap;  
* a defined funding mechanism or administrative path;  
* a project-specific release policy as required by the Release and Publication Policy;  
* a defined reporting expectation, at least annual;  
* any necessary conflict management steps;  
* any necessary external agreement path identified, if work will be performed outside OMSF.

If some elements remain to be finalized, the Board may approve the project conditionally, provided the conditions are clear and material funding is not disbursed until they are met.

## **10\. Forms of Approval**

The Board may approve a project in one of the following ways:

### **10.1 Full Approval**

The project is approved with a defined scope, budget, and implementation path.

### **10.2 Conditional Approval**

The project is approved subject to stated conditions, such as:

* final budget clarification;  
* confirmation of an external institution’s participation;  
* completion of release-policy documentation;  
* conflict-of-interest management steps;  
* OMSF administrative or legal review.

### **10.3 Pilot or Exploratory Approval**

The project is approved on a limited pilot basis with a defined cap on spending, time, or scope, after which further Board action is required for continuation or expansion.

### **10.4 Deferral**

The Board may defer action and request revision, additional information, budget alignment, or a different project structure.

## **11\. Budgeting and Spending Authority**

### **11.1 Board Approval of Budget**

The Board shall approve a high-level budget sufficient to realize the Strategic Goals while ensuring the long-term financial health of RSS, as required by the Charter.

### **11.2 Delegated Spending Authority**

Within a Board-approved budget, the Board may delegate spending authority to the Director for categories or thresholds it defines.

### **11.3 Major Expenditures**

Expenditures outside delegated authority, or otherwise reserved to the Board, require Board approval.

### **11.4 No Spending Without Lawful Path**

No project may receive material support unless OMSF or another authorized administrative mechanism can lawfully and practically administer the funding or resource commitment.

## **12\. Projects Involving Members or Sponsors**

Member and Partner participation in RSS includes the opportunity to recommend strategic directions, projects, and priorities. That said, a Member or Partner request does not automatically entitle that Member or Partner to funding, dedicated engineering work, or project approval.

Where a proposed Consortium-Funded Project responds to a Member or Partner need, the Board should consider:

* whether the work also advances RSS’s broader Mission;  
* whether the outputs can be maintained and released consistent with RSS policy;  
* whether the request would unduly skew priorities away from the broader community; and  
* whether the proposed scope belongs in ordinary membership support, a Consortium-Funded Project, or a separate written arrangement.

No project should be approved on terms that give a Member or Partner ownership of Consortium intellectual property or control over scientific conclusions.

## **13\. Projects Involving RSS Leadership or Affiliated Institutions**

If a proposed project or allocation would materially benefit:

* a sitting Board member;  
* the Director;  
* a Board member’s or Director’s institution;  
* a Board member’s or Director’s laboratory or research group; or  
* a closely related project in which such a person has a material interest,

then the conflict-of-interest provisions of the Charter apply in full.

At a minimum:

* the conflict must be disclosed;  
* the conflicted person must recuse themselves from voting and deliberation, except to provide factual information if requested;  
* the Board record should reflect the recusal and the basis for approval;  
* the project remains subject to annual reporting under the Charter and applicable policies.

The existence of such a connection does not automatically bar approval, but it requires heightened care.

## **14\. Funding Mechanisms**

RSS may support approved projects through one or more mechanisms, including:

* direct employment through OMSF;  
* direct contractor agreements through OMSF;  
* subawards or institutional agreements, where permitted;  
* fellowships, stipends, or trainee support mechanisms, where permitted;  
* reimbursements or pass-through arrangements, where lawful and administratively appropriate;  
* direct payment for approved infrastructure or service costs.

The appropriate mechanism should be selected based on legal feasibility, administrative efficiency, conflict management, institutional constraints, and project needs.

No policy preference is created merely by listing these mechanisms.

## **15\. Project Oversight**

### **15.1 Director Oversight**

Unless the Board specifies otherwise, the Director is responsible for day-to-day oversight of approved Consortium-Funded Projects, including tracking scope, budget use, progress, and whether issues should be elevated to the Board.

### **15.2 Board Oversight**

The Board retains authority to review the status of any Consortium-Funded Project, modify or curtail future support, impose additional reporting, or terminate project support prospectively where justified.

### **15.3 Technical Direction**

The Board’s oversight role does not displace Developer technical authority as established in the Charter.

## **16\. Reporting**

Each Consortium-Funded Project shall provide at least annual reporting to the Board, consistent with the Charter and the Release and Publication Policy.

Unless otherwise specified, the report should summarize:

* personnel effort supported by RSS;  
* progress against goals;  
* use of Consortium-administered funds or resources;  
* next steps or remaining milestones;  
* any significant release, publication, staffing, or compliance issues;  
* any material deviation from the approved scope or budget.

The Board may require more frequent reporting where appropriate.

## **17\. Changes in Scope, Budget, or Duration**

A Consortium-Funded Project must return to the Board for further approval if there is a material proposed change in:

* scope;  
* budget;  
* duration;  
* lead institution or performance structure;  
* release posture;  
* conflict profile;  
* expected outputs.

Minor operational adjustments within approved scope and delegated authority do not require Board action.

## **18\. Suspension, Termination, or Wind-Down of Projects**

The Board may suspend, terminate, or wind down support for a Consortium-Funded Project where justified by:

* budgetary necessity;  
* mission misalignment;  
* failure to make adequate progress;  
* unresolved compliance or legal concerns;  
* unmanaged conflicts of interest;  
* failure to satisfy approval conditions;  
* inability of OMSF to administer the project lawfully or practically;  
* strategic reprioritization.

Where feasible, the Board should seek an orderly wind-down that respects existing commitments, project integrity, contributor relationships, and the open-source mission of RSS.

Suspension or termination of support does not by itself determine ownership of resulting work, which remains governed by the Charter, applicable agreements, and the Contribution and Intellectual Property Policy.

## **19\. Emergency or Time-Sensitive Funding Decisions**

Where an urgent funding or project decision must be made to avoid material harm to RSS and ordinary Board timing is impractical, the Board may act using the Charter’s emergency decision procedures.

Emergency procedures should be used sparingly and not as a substitute for ordinary project review.

## **20\. Records**

The Director and OMSF should maintain reasonable records sufficient to document:

* approved Consortium-Funded Projects;  
* budgets and budget caps;  
* approval conditions, if any;  
* project-specific release-policy completion;  
* reporting submissions;  
* conflict disclosures and recusals related to funding decisions;  
* major Board decisions regarding continuation, modification, or termination of project support.

These records may be maintained through board packets, minutes, project files, or other ordinary governance records.

## **21\. Special Arrangements**

Nothing in this Policy prohibits RSS and OMSF from entering into special project-specific arrangements, provided they are:

* consistent with the Charter;  
* approved through appropriate RSS and OMSF processes;  
* documented in writing; and  
* not used to circumvent conflict, release, or governance rules.

## **22\. Review and Amendment**

The Board may amend this Policy from time to time, consistent with the Charter, OMSF policy, and applicable law.

The Board should review this Policy periodically in light of practical experience, sponsor relationships, conflict-management needs, and institutional feedback.
